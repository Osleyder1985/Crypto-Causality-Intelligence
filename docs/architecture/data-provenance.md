# Data Provenance and Temporal Semantics

## Purpose

Historical research must establish not only what a record says, but also when the underlying event occurred, when information was published, and when that information could have been used by the system.

## Timestamp definitions

| Field | Definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `event_time` | Time the real-world event occurred | May be unknown, approximate, or represented as an interval. |
| `published_at` | Time a source made a document or announcement public | Preserve source-provided precision and timezone when available. |
| `captured_at` | Time the system retrieved or captured the source content | Record retrieval failures and delayed captures separately. |
| `ingested_at` | Time the record was committed to the platform's managed data store | Generated by the ingestion system. |
| `available_at` | Earliest defensible time the record was available for use under the platform's declared information policy | Must not be backdated merely because the underlying event happened earlier. |

These fields are not interchangeable. Some records will not have every timestamp. Unknown values must remain unknown rather than being inferred without an explicit, documented method.

## Point-in-time correctness

The default research policy is strict point-in-time availability.

A record may be used for an analysis cutoff `T` only if its `available_at` is known and less than or equal to `T`, subject to the dataset's declared availability policy.

For source publications, `published_at` may establish public availability when independently trustworthy and sufficiently precise. The platform's `captured_at` establishes when the system actually possessed the content. If publication time is unavailable, disputed, or unreliable, use the defensible capture/availability time rather than estimating an earlier time.

For market data, availability depends on provider delivery and finalization behavior. A candle's interval end is not proof that the finalized candle was available at that exact instant. Record capture/availability and provisional/final status separately.

## Event-time uncertainty

Events should support exact timestamps where supported by evidence, approximate timestamps with stated precision, earliest/latest bounds when only an interval is known, and unknown time when no defensible estimate exists.

Do not convert a date-only event into an arbitrary midnight timestamp without preserving its original precision. Preserve the source's original timezone and representation where relevant, while normalizing machine-comparable timestamps to UTC.

## Timestamp representation

- Store instants in UTC using an unambiguous representation (for example, RFC 3339 with a UTC offset).
- Preserve original timestamp text, timezone, and precision when supplied by the source.
- Distinguish instants from date-only values and uncertain time intervals.
- Use explicit interval boundaries for candles and aggregates, with a documented convention such as half-open intervals `[start, end)`.
- Record clock source/synchronization assumptions for ingestion infrastructure where operationally relevant.

## Provenance requirements

Every retained record should be traceable to source/provider and source-native identifier, URL/endpoint/dataset or other retrievable reference, capture and ingestion records, original payload or legally permitted representation, content hash and algorithm, schema and normalization version, revision/supersession relationship, and transformation runs that produced derived records.

A hash demonstrates byte-level integrity relative to the hashed representation; it does not prove that a source is authentic or that its claims are true.

## Reconstructing what was known at time T

A point-in-time dataset must be generated from records satisfying the availability cutoff and selected revision policy. The query or dataset manifest must record cutoff timestamp, availability policy, source and record selection, treatment of revisions and late-arriving data, schema and transformation versions, exclusions, and unresolved timestamp uncertainty.

Later corrections must not silently rewrite a previously generated historical research dataset. A new reconstruction may incorporate later corrections, but it must be distinguishable from the earlier point-in-time view.

## Example

An event occurs at 10:00 UTC. An official document is published at 10:15 UTC and captured at 10:17 UTC.

- A simulation at 10:05 cannot use the document.
- A simulation at 10:16 may use it only if the publication timestamp is trustworthy and the policy permits public publication time as availability.
- If the platform requires actual system capture, the document is usable from 10:17.
- The event's 10:00 occurrence time does not make the later document available at 10:00.

## Leakage prevention

- Never backdate records based on event time.
- Exclude records with unknown or untrustworthy availability from strict point-in-time datasets unless a conservative availability rule is applied and documented.
- Fit imputers, scalers, feature selection, and other learned preprocessing only on the permitted training window.
- Prevent revised/finalized values from leaking into earlier snapshots.
- Preserve dataset manifests and cutoff policies for every backtest or research run.
